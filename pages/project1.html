Emergence of a context for musicking with children with ASD

This project is a long-term collaboration with two non-profit organizations and children with autistic spectrum disorder (ASD). It explores how to design an inclusive musicking context where Accessible Digital Musical Instruments (ADMIs) become one element among others in a broader social ecosystem.

Context

In recent years, there has been a growing interest in designing musical instruments and interfaces that bring musicking to people with health conditions and impairments [1]. Musicking is defined as the process through which one takes part in music performance, whether by performing, listening, dancing, practicing or rehearsing [2].

This area of research, called Accessible Digital Musical Instrument (ADMI), promotes the accessibility of musical control interfaces used in electronic music, inclusive music practices and music therapy [1]. The aim is both to facilitate musical expression among these audiences and to use musical performance as a form of care.

Approach

Recent studies have highlighted the value of participatory approaches, in which people with disabilities are involved in the creation of musical instruments so that these instruments incorporate their specific characteristics. Their extension to collective situations remains a challenge.

Participatory and inclusive design approaches to ADMI are motivated by the social model of disability [1, 3, 4], which views disability in terms of factors external to the individual and non-inclusive social attitudes that create barriers for people with disabilities.

Without explicitly drawing on these theories, the Disability Art movement [7] set out to challenge the negative view of disability as deviance from a certain normality and adopted a social model of disability to highlight social, political and economical obstacles faced by disabled people [8].

Research questions

In line with the political conceptions of disability that have emerged from Disability Arts, the association Brutpop develops alternative musical practices with disabled people. In these practices, which we call Brut music, technology and ADMIs are seen as components that enable access to musical practice without requiring musical expertise.

We study how interaction designs for musical expression, dedicated to children and young adults with ASD, can be shaped through the co-agency of social contexts, researchers, caregivers, children and technology.

We also study how a participatory action research methodology and the development of Brut music practices can help design a social context that supports the emergence of an inclusive musicking ecosystem.

Contribution

This participatory action research project is a long-term and on-going collaboration with two non-profit organizations and non-verbal children with ASD. It explores different forms of Brut music practices in an "in the wild" context [10], where activities happen in situ and involve people and artefacts as part of daily practices and organisational routines.

We took a broader view to set up a space where experiences of musical expression could emerge, with the aim of identifying guidelines and socio-material design recommendations for other socio-cultural environments and for research into ADMIs.
